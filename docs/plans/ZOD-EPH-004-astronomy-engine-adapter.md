# ZOD-EPH-004 astronomy-engine `EphemerisPort` adapter (backup and oracle), Android and host: Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:executing-plans (default, sequential). Nothing here is independent enough for subagents: each task consumes the previous task's output (vendored source, then the pure math, then the binding, then the adapter, then the device runs).

**Goal:** Deliver an `EphemerisPort` over the astronomy-engine C library (FFI, Android and host-testable logic) for the ten bodies, within 1 arcminute of JPL Horizons (ACC-2), with the longitude speed derived **analytically** from the library's own position and velocity vectors, and prove how good that speed is against Swiss Ephemeris, including at planetary stations.

**Architecture:** astronomy-engine's two files (`astronomy.c`, `astronomy.h`) are vendored byte-for-byte under `native/astronomy_c/upstream/`. A small project-authored C shim (`native/astronomy_c/zodiann_astronomy_shim.c`) calls the library and returns flat doubles (apparent longitude, geocentric position and velocity in the true ecliptic of date), so the FFI surface has no struct-by-value. A build hook (native assets, Android only, as in ZOD-FND-003) compiles both. All arithmetic that is not a library call (the longitude rate, normalization, range check, node, result mapping) is pure Dart in `ephemeris_astronomy` and is tested on the host with literal vectors; the native source is injected behind an interface. Accuracy is proved on the Android emulator by integration tests in `apps/astro_mobile`.

**Tech Stack:** Dart 3.14 / Flutter 3.50.0 (`.fvmrc`), `hooks ^2.2.0`, `code_assets ^2.0.0`, `native_toolchain_c ^0.19.4`, `logging ^1.3.0`, `ffi ^2.1.4`, dev `ffigen ^23.0.0` (the versions ZOD-FND-003 proved; libclang already installed), Android NDK 28.2.13676358, `integration_test`, astronomy-engine C (MIT).

**Spec:** `assets/zodiann-specification.md`: "Data sources, in order of priority" (line 350, astronomy-engine row), ACC-2 (line 382), ARCH-4 (line 552), CRS-A1 (line 373), R-2, NFR-A1 (apparent, true equinox of date). Task file: `docs/development/computation-engine/ZOD-EPH-004-spike-implement-the-astronomy-engine-ephemerisport-adapter-backup.md`. Dependencies: ZOD-FND-002 and ZOD-EPH-001 (both done). Build pattern and findings to reuse: `docs/spikes/ZOD-FND-003-swiss-ephemeris-ffi.md`, `docs/plans/ZOD-FND-003-swiss-ephemeris-ffi-spike-android.md`.

## Global Constraints

- **CRS-A1 (spec line 373):** "The longitude speed (degrees per day) comes directly from the ephemeris, and retrograde is a negative speed." **Owner decision, 2026-11-21: no finite differences, and no finite-difference exception.** The speed is derived analytically from the state vectors the library provides (D1). The adapter and every document call it that, and none claims it equals the derivative of the fully apparent longitude: the derivatives of the light-time and aberration corrections are omitted (D1 lists what else).
- **Do not modify the existing contract** (`EphemerisPort` and its documentation, `astro_core`) without first documenting the exact proposed change and its implications (Task 8 writes it up; nothing is edited in `astro_core`).
- **ACC-2 and the speed comparison are separate acceptance criteria** (D6, D7). The Horizons longitude bound is 1 arcminute for every body; Pluto's accuracy is also measured and reported on its own.
- **Stop-and-report rule (owner):** if the speed comparison against Swiss Ephemeris is insufficient by the thresholds in D7, **stop and report the findings**. Do not switch to finite differences, do not weaken CRS-A1, and do not silently change the speed formulation or the thresholds. If the Task 1 reading shows `Astronomy_GeoMoonState`'s velocity is a numerical derivative, stop and report the source findings; do not implement the Moon path or add a finite-difference exception without a separate owner decision.
- **Execution is sequential**, each task completed and verified before the next. No production fallback wiring is implemented (D5). `EphemerisPort`, `astro_core` and the specification contract are not modified.
- **Scope: Android and host only.** iOS stays deferred (no macOS access, no spending, owner constraint 2026-11-20). The task file does not mention iOS, but R-7 and ARCH-6 require it for the FFI binding of the primary source; this task records the iOS checklist for astronomy-engine in the findings and does not claim it verified.
- **Contract (`EphemerisPort`, read 2026-11-21):** UTC-only (`ArgumentError` otherwise); physical bodies geocentric, apparent, longitude on the true ecliptic and equinox of date, no extra precession correction; `Body.node` answered by `meanAscendingNode` for any instant, **before** the covered-range check, via `with MeanAscendingNodeFormula` (no re-implementation, and the `125.04452` and `1934.136261` literals stay out of this package); out of range gives `OutOfCoveredRange`, never an approximate position (R-2); results normalized with `normalizeDegrees`; every `EphemerisSuccess` holds an `EclipticPosition(longitude, speed)`.
- **Upstream unchanged:** the astronomy-engine files are copied byte-for-byte with exact version and origin recorded; its MIT notice is kept (`LICENSE`). The project-authored shim is AGPL-3.0-only like the rest.

## Review Focus

1. **A longitude that is right says nothing about the speed.** Expected: speed is tested by itself, against Swiss Ephemeris, not inferred from the Horizons longitude pass (Tasks 2 and 6).
2. **Stations.** At a station the true speed is about zero and the sign flips; a small speed error moves the sign flip by hours or days. Expected: the retrograde sign agrees with Swiss wherever `|Swiss speed|` exceeds the fixed D7(a) bound for that body; the offset of the zero crossing is measured per station and reported (Task 6).
3. **Pluto.** Astronomy Engine models Pluto with its own gravity simulation and states no Pluto accuracy. Expected: Pluto's longitude error and speed error are reported on their own line, and Pluto is never hidden inside a "worst of all bodies" figure (Tasks 5 and 6).
4. **The Moon and the Sun are not planets in the library.** The Sun and planets use `Astronomy_GeoVector` with aberration; the Moon uses `Astronomy_EclipticGeoMoon` and `Astronomy_GeoMoonState`; `Astronomy_SunPosition` is only the Sun's independent diagnostic. Expected: each of the ten bodies is exercised by its own device assertion; a wrong frame (J2000 instead of date) would shift every planet by a few tenths of a degree, which the ACC-2 test catches (Task 5).
5. **`360.0` after normalization.** A tiny negative longitude must not become exactly `360.0` (`EclipticPosition` rejects it). Expected: a unit test feeds `-1e-15` through the adapter (Task 4).
6. **The out-of-range edge.** Expected: the instants at the exact start and end of `coveredRange` succeed, one microsecond outside returns `OutOfCoveredRange`, and the node still succeeds outside (Task 4).
7. **Release builds drop unreferenced native assets** (ZOD-FND-003 gotcha 8). Expected: the findings say the first use by app code brings the library in; this task adds no production wiring that would hide that (Task 7).

## Decisions

Owner decisions made (2026-11-21): speed analytic, option (b); no finite differences; ACC-2 separate; Pluto independent; stop and report if insufficient; scope Android and host. **Plan approved in principle by the owner on 2026-11-21 with these further decisions, recorded below: D4 approved; D5 deferral approved with ZOD-EPH-002 as the owner of automatic source selection; D6 Option A; D7 thresholds approved except the Moon's (diagnostic first, then the owner confirms and freezes it); the Moon-velocity gate is a stop-and-report; the eight-double buffer correction.** The remaining items are this plan's calls.

- **D1 (speed derivation, owner decision; the formulation is this plan's call).** For each physical body the shim returns the **apparent geocentric position** `r` (the library's light-time-corrected, aberrated vector) and the **geocentric velocity** `v` (the library's velocity of the body minus the Earth's, both from `Astronomy_BaryState`; the Moon from `Astronomy_GeoMoonState`), both rotated into the true ecliptic of date with `Astronomy_Rotation_EQJ_ECT` and `Astronomy_RotateVector`. **For the Sun and every planet the longitude returned by the shim is `atan2(y, x)` of that same rotated `r`**, so longitude, position and velocity share one frame by construction (plan review I1); `Astronomy_Ecliptic` is not called. **Moon, two points the Task 1 reading must settle before any code (plan review I2):** (i) if `Astronomy_GeoMoonState`'s velocity is itself a numerical derivative inside the library, that conflicts with the owner's "no finite differences" decision. **Owner ruling, 2026-11-21: stop and report the source findings. Do not implement the Moon path, and do not introduce a finite-difference exception, without a separate owner decision;** (ii) if the library's Moon position carries no aberration (up to about 20.5 arcseconds), D1 and the findings say so, and the Moon's maximum longitude error is reported on its own line. The speed is `λ' = (x·vy − y·vx) / (x² + y²)` converted from radians to degrees per day, in Dart. **What it omits, and the docs must say so:** the time derivative of the light-time correction, the time derivative of the aberration correction, and the rotation rate of the ecliptic-of-date frame (precession about 3.8e-5 degrees per day, nutation smaller); `v` is the instantaneous velocity at `t`, not at the light-time-retarded instant. **This is therefore not the derivative of the fully apparent longitude.** Whether it is good enough is decided by D7, not asserted. If D7 fails, the plan stops; a refinement (velocity at the retarded instant) is an option to put to the owner in the report, not to apply silently.
- **D2 (flat C shim, project-authored).** `int zodiann_body_state(int body, double ut_days, double* out)`. **`out` points to exactly eight doubles (`out[0]` through `out[7]`).** The shim header defines `#define ZODIANN_BODY_STATE_LENGTH 8`, documents that the caller owns a buffer of that length, and the shim writes only `out[0..7]` and writes nothing on an invalid `body`. Dart mirrors it as `const int bodyStateLength = 8` in `body_state.dart` and allocates that many doubles (owner correction 2026-11-21: an earlier draft of this plan allocated seven). Layout: `out[0]` apparent longitude (degrees, true ecliptic of date), `out[1..3]` `r` (AU), `out[4..6]` `v` (AU per day), `out[7]` a diagnostic longitude from an independent library path (the Sun only: `Astronomy_SunPosition`; the Moon and the planets: `NaN`, because the Moon's `out[0]` already is the `Astronomy_EclipticGeoMoon` value and Dart compares it with `atan2` of `out[1..2]`, and the planets have no independent path); returns `0` or the library's `astro_status_t` code; `body` is the shim id (`sun` 0 … `pluto` 9) mapped to `astro_body_t` inside the shim; Dart passes it through an explicit `Body`-to-id table (not `Body.index`), pinned by a host test (plan review M2); `ut_days` is days since J2000 UT (`Astronomy_TimeFromDays`). Reason: avoids struct-by-value across FFI (a spike risk) and keeps upstream untouched. Trade-off: about 80 lines of our own C, tested only on a device.
- **D3 (Android-only hook, as ZOD-FND-003 D3).** `hook/build.dart` builds only for `OS.android`; the host loads no native code. Host tests inject a fake source. Consequence stated up front: **ACC-2 and the speed comparison cannot run in `Core` or `Full suite` CI**; they are device runs recorded in the findings. Wiring a release-blocking run is ZOD-TST-017's job. (A host build with the local `gcc` or CI's compiler is possible later and is not done here.)
- **D4 (covered range).** `UtcRange(1899-12-31T12:00:00Z, 2100-01-01T12:00:00Z)`, the T = ±1 window of `packages/astro_core/test/fixtures/reference_dates.json`, the only span ACC-2 is proved over. The library claims millennia; the adapter does not claim what it did not verify. **Approved by the owner, 2026-11-21.** A wider range later is a one-line change after more verification.
- **D5 (fallback wiring is not in this task). Approved by the owner, 2026-11-21.** The task text says "wire as the automatic fallback when the Swiss Ephemeris FFI binding is unavailable". The Swiss adapter (ZOD-EPH-002) does not exist yet and is blocked on the iOS half of ZOD-FND-003, so there is nothing to fall back from. **ZOD-EPH-002 owns the automatic source-selection behavior**, since it defines the primary adapter and its availability. This task delivers the DI-swappable adapter only (acceptance criterion 2, proved by a test), implements **no production fallback wiring**, and writes the carry-over as a Notes line in the ZOD-EPH-002 task file (and in PROGRESS). No `FallbackEphemerisPort` is added to `astro_core`.
- **D6 (ACC-2 fixture and ownership). Option A approved by the owner, 2026-11-21 (plan review I5).** The task criterion says "same reference-date set as T-EPH-1", but T-EPH-1 (ZOD-TST-001) is not built and is blocked, and ZOD-TST-005 ("Author the astronomy-engine backup comparison suite (ACC-2)", depends on EPH-004 and TST-001) owns the T-EPH-1 run against this adapter. Option A, approved: this task proves ACC-2's bound with its own Horizons fixture over **all 53 instants of `reference_dates.json`** (add-only file, not edited; it names itself the shared T-EPH-1 set) for the ten physical bodies, fetched by `tool/fetch_horizons_reference.dart` with the ZOD-FND-003 query settings (quantity 31, `CENTER=500@399`, UT; one batch per body), and the criterion text is amended in the task file to "proved on this task's own device test over the 53 `reference_dates.json` instants; the T-EPH-1 suite run is tracked by ZOD-TST-005" (the pattern used for earlier tasks), with a Notes line added to ZOD-TST-005. Option B (a 10-instant fixture, no amendment) was not chosen by the owner: it would have weakened the criterion silently and duplicated TST-005. Bound: 60 arcseconds, asserted per body, with each body's maximum printed on its own line (Moon and Pluto included).
- **D7 (speed acceptance). Thresholds approved by the owner on 2026-11-21 for Mercury (0.0012 °/day), Venus (0.00045 °/day) and the Sun, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune and Pluto (0.0003 °/day). The Moon's threshold is NOT approved:** run a diagnostic measurement first, propose a threshold from the observed maximum plus a small, justified margin, obtain the owner's confirmation, freeze it, and only then run the formal pass/fail test (Task 6). **The other bodies' thresholds are never changed on the strength of diagnostic results.** Reference: Swiss Ephemeris `swe_calc_ut` speed with the data files (`SEFLG_SWIEPH | SEFLG_SPEED`), on the emulator, same process. **Expected error, per body (plan review round 2, I2).** Three omitted terms add up: the aberration derivative (about 9.8e-5 °/day), the frame rotation (precession about 3.8e-5 °/day), and the light-time retardation term, which exists because `r` is the apparent (retarded) position while `v` is the instantaneous velocity at `t`. The reviewer's circular-orbit model (light time only, scratch script, not in the repository) gave that third term as Mercury about 6.8e-4, Venus about 1.95e-4, Mars about 1.7e-5, Jupiter about 7e-6 °/day (outer planets smaller still). Expected totals: Mercury about 7e-4 to 8e-4, Venus about 3e-4, the Sun, Mars and the outer planets at or below about 1.5e-4, the Moon about 1e-4 to 2e-4 (aberration and frame only, if the library omits Moon aberration). The model is an estimate, not a measurement. Sufficient means all of: (a) `|astronomy speed − Swiss speed|` ≤ **1.2e-3 °/day** for Mercury, ≤ **4.5e-4** for Venus, ≤ **3e-4** for the Sun, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune and Pluto, and, for the Moon, the figure the owner freezes after the diagnostic run (the earlier 0.005 was a placeholder and is not an acceptance threshold; the diagnostic run asserts nothing for the Moon), at every sampled instant. (b) the sign of the speed (retrograde) equals Swiss's at every sampled instant where `|Swiss speed|` exceeds the **fixed bound of (a)** for that body (not the measured maximum, which would widen its own exemption); (c) at each station the zero-crossing instant of the astronomy speed is within **1 day** of Swiss's, and the offset is reported. Expectation stated in advance: the offset is about 0.1 to 0.5 day for Uranus, Neptune and Pluto (speed slope near a station is about 2.7e-4 °/day² for Neptune), under an hour for Mercury; an outer-planet offset above 1 day is a stop-and-report signal. If any criterion fails: **stop and report the measurements** (Global Constraints); the speed formulation and the thresholds are not changed silently. The light-time term makes Mercury (and Venus near its limit) the likeliest to fail with the D1 formulation. A refinement exists (take `v` at the light-time-retarded instant `t − τ`, with `τ = |r| / c`, using `Astronomy_BaryState` at that instant; not a finite difference, still library vectors), but it is a different formulation from the one approved, **the owner has not approved it, and it is not applied**; it would be put to the owner in the stop-and-report that a failure produces.
- **D8 (stations and sample set).** Sample instants: the 53 of D6 plus, for each of Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune and Pluto, every station between 1900 and 2100 found by scanning Swiss speed daily and bisecting to a minute, each sampled at the station and at ±1, ±5 and ±20 days. The Sun adds 20 instants spread over 2026. **The Moon uses a dense set, because its speed and its error vary over about a month (perigee and apogee) and 20 instants cover one lunation unevenly, so a threshold frozen from them would pass on the same instants and say little about the rest (plan review, revision cycle I3):** every 6 hours from 2025-01-01T00:00Z to 2027-01-01T00:00Z (2,921 instants, more than 25 perigee and apogee passes), plus the 53 reference dates. The Moon-only set is this plan's call; it does not touch any other body's thresholds or samples. The test states the count and has an explicit timeout. **Scale:** the eight planets have roughly 2,000 stations in 1900 to 2100 (Mercury alone about 1,260), so about 14,000 station samples plus the daily Swiss scan; Task 6 states the expected count, sets an explicit test timeout, and may thin Mercury and Venus to every fifth station if the timeout is exceeded (recorded in the findings, not silent).
- **D9 (body ids, Horizons commands).** Swiss ids: sun 0, moon 1, mercury 2, venus 3, mars 4, jupiter 5, saturn 6, uranus 7, neptune 8, pluto 9 (`swephexp.h`). Horizons: 10, 301, 199, 299, 499, 599, 699, 799, 899, 999 (planet centers; Pluto is 999).
