# ZOD-EPH-004 astronomy-engine `EphemerisPort` adapter (backup and oracle), Android and host: Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:executing-plans (default, sequential). Nothing here is independent enough for subagents: each task consumes the previous task's output (vendored source, then the pure math, then the binding, then the adapter, then the device runs).

**Goal:** Deliver an `EphemerisPort` over the astronomy-engine C library (FFI, Android and host-testable logic) for the ten bodies, within 1 arcminute of JPL Horizons (ACC-2), with the longitude speed derived **analytically** from the library's own position and velocity vectors, and prove how good that speed is against Swiss Ephemeris, including at planetary stations.

**Architecture:** astronomy-engine's two files (`astronomy.c`, `astronomy.h`) are vendored byte-for-byte under `native/astronomy_c/upstream/`. A small project-authored C shim (`native/astronomy_c/zodiann_astronomy_shim.c`) calls the library and returns flat doubles (apparent longitude, geocentric position and velocity in the true ecliptic of date), so the FFI surface has no struct-by-value. A build hook (native assets, Android only, as in ZOD-FND-003) compiles both. All arithmetic that is not a library call (the longitude rate, normalization, range check, node, result mapping) is pure Dart in `ephemeris_astronomy` and is tested on the host with literal vectors; the native source is injected behind an interface. Accuracy is proved on the Android emulator by integration tests in `apps/astro_mobile`.

**Tech Stack:** Dart 3.14 / Flutter 3.50.0 (`.fvmrc`), `hooks ^2.2.0`, `code_assets ^2.0.0`, `native_toolchain_c ^0.19.4`, `logging ^1.3.0`, `ffi ^2.1.4`, dev `ffigen ^23.0.0` (the versions ZOD-FND-003 proved; libclang already installed), Android NDK 28.2.13676358, `integration_test`, astronomy-engine C (MIT).

**Spec:** `assets/zodiann-specification.md`: "Data sources, in order of priority" (line 350, astronomy-engine row), ACC-2 (line 382), ARCH-4 (line 552), CRS-A1 (line 373), R-2, NFR-A1 (apparent, true equinox of date). Task file: `docs/development/computation-engine/ZOD-EPH-004-spike-implement-the-astronomy-engine-ephemerisport-adapter-backup.md`. Dependencies: ZOD-FND-002 and ZOD-EPH-001 (both done). Build pattern and findings to reuse: `docs/spikes/ZOD-FND-003-swiss-ephemeris-ffi.md`, `docs/plans/ZOD-FND-003-swiss-ephemeris-ffi-spike-android.md`.

## Global Constraints

The adapter and every document call it that, and none claims it equals the derivative of the fully apparent longitude: the derivatives of the light-time and aberration corrections are omitted (D1 lists what else).
- **Do not modify the existing contract** (`EphemerisPort` and its documentation, `astro_core`) without first documenting the exact proposed change and its implications (Task 8 writes it up; nothing is edited in `astro_core`).
- **ACC-2 and the speed comparison are separate acceptance criteria** (D6, D7). The Horizons longitude bound is 1 arcminute for every body; Pluto's accuracy is also measured and reported on its own.
- **Stop-and-report rule (owner):** if the speed comparison against Swiss Ephemeris is insufficient by the thresholds in D7, **stop and report the findings**. Do not switch to finite differences, do not weaken CRS-A1, and do not silently change the speed formulation or the thresholds. The Moon-velocity gate was triggered by the Task 1 reading and is resolved by the owner's Option 2 decision of 2026-11-21 (D1); no finite-difference exception exists. **Nothing is implemented until this revised plan has passed its focused review and the owner has approved it.**
- **No finite differences, including inherited ones (owner, 2026-11-21).** The shim and the adapter use no library function that computes a velocity by differencing positions. The Moon's velocity is the project-authored analytic derivative of upstream's `CalcMoon` series (D1, `docs/spikes/ZOD-EPH-004-moon-derivative.md`); `Astronomy_GeoMoonState`, `Astronomy_GeoEmbState` and `Astronomy_BaryState` / `Astronomy_HelioState` for `BODY_MOON` / `BODY_EMB` are never called (host guard, Task 3 Step 1a). The upstream Moon position code stays unmodified and distinct from our velocity code. If a complete and verifiable derivative cannot be achieved, stop and report; no fallback to differences and no silent approximation.
- **Execution is sequential**, each task completed and verified before the next. No production fallback wiring is implemented (D5). `EphemerisPort`, `astro_core` and the specification contract are not modified.
- **Scope: Android and host only.** iOS stays deferred (no macOS access, no spending, owner constraint 2026-11-20). The task file does not mention iOS, but R-7 and ARCH-6 require it for the FFI binding of the primary source; this task records the iOS checklist for astronomy-engine in the findings and does not claim it verified.

## Review Focus

1. **A longitude that is right says nothing about the speed.** Expected: speed is tested by itself, against Swiss Ephemeris, not inferred from the Horizons longitude pass (Tasks 2 and 6).
2. **Stations.** At a station the true speed is about zero and the sign flips; a small speed error moves the sign flip by hours or days. Expected: the retrograde sign agrees with Swiss wherever `|Swiss speed|` exceeds the fixed D7(a) bound for that body; the offset of the zero crossing is measured per station and reported (Task 6).
3. **Pluto.** Astronomy Engine models Pluto with its own gravity simulation and states no Pluto accuracy. Expected: Pluto's longitude error and speed error are reported on their own line, and Pluto is never hidden inside a "worst of all bodies" figure (Tasks 5 and 6).
4. **The Moon and the Sun are not planets in the library.** The Sun and planets use `Astronomy_GeoVector` with aberration; the Moon uses `Astronomy_EclipticGeoMoon` for its longitude and our analytic derivative for its velocity (`Astronomy_GeoMoonState` is banned); `Astronomy_SunPosition` is only the Sun's independent diagnostic. Expected: each of the ten bodies is exercised by its own device assertion; a wrong frame (J2000 instead of date) would shift every planet by a few tenths of a degree, which the ACC-2 test catches (Task 5).
5. **`360.0` after normalization.** A tiny negative longitude must not become exactly `360.0` (`EclipticPosition` rejects it). Expected: a unit test feeds `-1e-15` through the adapter (Task 4).
6. **The out-of-range edge.** Expected: the instants at the exact start and end of `coveredRange` succeed, one microsecond outside returns `OutOfCoveredRange`, and the node still succeeds outside (Task 4).
7. **A transcription slip in the Moon series, or a wrong derivative, would look like theory error.** Expected: the value regression against upstream's `CalcMoon` (1e-11 radians and relative) and the integral identity (1e-9 degrees per interval) separate those cases from the Swiss comparison (Task 3 Step 1b); the table of 114 terms is generated, not typed (Step 1a).
8. **The Earth observer's velocity may carry the Moon's reflex** (D10): a 27.3-day oscillation of up to about 4e-4/d °/day in the other bodies' speed. Expected: if it shows in the Swiss comparison, the report names it (E1), and the owner decides E2.
9. **Release builds drop unreferenced native assets** (ZOD-FND-003 gotcha 8). Expected: the findings say the first use by app code brings the library in; this task adds no production wiring that would hide that (Task 7).

## Decisions

Owner decisions made (2026-11-21): speed analytic, option (b); no finite differences; ACC-2 separate; Pluto independent; stop and report if insufficient; scope Android and host. **Plan approved in principle by the owner on 2026-11-21 with these further decisions, recorded below: D4 approved; D5 deferral approved with ZOD-EPH-002 as the owner of automatic source selection; D6 Option A; D7 thresholds approved except the Moon's (diagnostic first, then the owner confirms and freezes it); the Moon-velocity gate is a stop-and-report; the eight-double buffer correction.** The remaining items are this plan's calls.

- **D1 (speed derivation, owner decision; the formulation is this plan's call).** For each physical body the shim returns the **apparent geocentric position** `r` (the library's light-time-corrected, aberrated vector) and the **geocentric velocity** `v` (for the Sun and the planets, the library's velocity of the body minus the Earth's, both from `Astronomy_BaryState`; **for the Moon, our own analytic derivative, see below**), both rotated into the true ecliptic of date with `Astronomy_Rotation_EQJ_ECT` and `Astronomy_RotateVector`. **For the Sun and every planet the longitude returned by the shim is `atan2(y, x)` of that same rotated `r`**, so longitude, position and velocity share one frame by construction (plan review I1); `Astronomy_Ecliptic` is not called. **The Moon (owner decision, 2026-11-21: Option 2).** The Task 1 source reading found that `Astronomy_GeoMoonState` derives the Moon's velocity as a central difference of `Astronomy_GeoMoon` (dt = 1e-5 days; astronomy.c line 2433). The owner ruled that this is not acceptable, no finite-difference exception is made (including one inherited from the upstream library), and the Moon's velocity is the **exact analytic derivative of upstream's `CalcMoon` series, computed by project-authored code in the C shim**. The derivation, the list of omitted terms and the feasibility assessment are in [`docs/spikes/ZOD-EPH-004-moon-derivative.md`](../spikes/ZOD-EPH-004-moon-derivative.md) (verdict: feasible). **Upstream versus ours, kept distinct everywhere (shim comments, adapter documentation, findings):** the Moon's *position* is upstream's (`CalcMoon`, and `Astronomy_EclipticGeoMoon` for `out[0]`); the Moon's *velocity* is project-authored (`native/astronomy_c/zodiann_moon_rate.c`, with its term table generated from the vendored source). The shim never calls `Astronomy_GeoMoonState`, `Astronomy_GeoEmbState`, or `Astronomy_BaryState` / `Astronomy_HelioState` with `BODY_MOON` or `BODY_EMB` (a host test scans the shim sources for those identifiers). The Moon's `r` and `v` are built in the ecliptic of the mean equinox of date from the series and its derivative, then rotated to ECT by the same fixed chain the library applies to the position (D2). The Moon's omitted terms differ from the other bodies': the rate of the nutation and obliquity rotation (about 5e-5 °/day at most) and the rate of ΔT (about 1e-7 to 3e-7 °/day) are omitted; the precession rate is **included** (the series arguments are measured from the moving equinox); the library's Moon position carries no aberration or light time, of the order of 1 arcsecond for the Moon (the earlier "about 20.5 arcseconds" figure was wrong for the Moon), and the Moon's maximum longitude error is reported on its own line. The speed is `λ' = (x·vy − y·vx) / (x² + y²)` converted from radians to degrees per day, in Dart. **What it omits, and the docs must say so:** the time derivative of the light-time correction, the time derivative of the aberration correction, and the rotation rate of the ecliptic-of-date frame (precession about 3.8e-5 degrees per day, nutation smaller); `v` is the instantaneous velocity at `t`, not at the light-time-retarded instant. **This is therefore not the derivative of the fully apparent longitude.** Whether it is good enough is decided by D7, not asserted. If D7 fails, the plan stops; a refinement (velocity at the retarded instant) is an option to put to the owner in the report, not to apply silently.
- **D2 (flat C shim, project-authored).** `int zodiann_body_state(int body, double ut_days, double* out)`. **`out` points to exactly eight doubles (`out[0]` through `out[7]`).** The shim header defines `#define ZODIANN_BODY_STATE_LENGTH 8`, documents that the caller owns a buffer of that length, and the shim writes only `out[0..7]` and writes nothing on an invalid `body`. Dart mirrors it as `const int bodyStateLength = 8` in `body_state.dart` and allocates that many doubles (owner correction 2026-11-21: an earlier draft of this plan allocated seven). Layout: `out[0]` apparent longitude (degrees, true ecliptic of date), `out[1..3]` `r` (AU), `out[4..6]` `v` (AU per day), `out[7]` a diagnostic longitude from an independent library path (the Sun only: `Astronomy_SunPosition`; the Moon and the planets: `NaN`. For the Moon, `out[0]` is upstream's `Astronomy_EclipticGeoMoon` value and `atan2(out[2], out[1])` comes from our value path, so Dart compares the two (they must agree to 1e-9 degrees, since both apply the same chain to the same series); the planets have no independent path); returns `0` or the library's `astro_status_t` code; `body` is the shim id (`sun` 0 … `pluto` 9) mapped to `astro_body_t` inside the shim; Dart passes it through an explicit `Body`-to-id table (not `Body.index`), pinned by a host test (plan review M2); `ut_days` is days since J2000 UT (`Astronomy_TimeFromDays`). Reason: avoids struct-by-value across FFI (a spike risk) and keeps upstream untouched. **Moon branch and build shape (owner decision 2026-11-21, Option 2).** The shim is one translation unit that `#include`s the unmodified `native/astronomy_c/upstream/astronomy.c` (this gives access to the static `CalcMoon` and the rotation helpers the Moon needs; the file is not edited; the manifest guard and the value-regression test make a version change fail loudly), so the hook compiles the shim alone. The Moon branch of `zodiann_body_state` is: `out[0]` = `Astronomy_EclipticGeoMoon(t).lon` (upstream, unchanged); `r` and `v` = project-authored, from `native/astronomy_c/zodiann_moon_rate.{c,h}` (the analytic derivative of the `CalcMoon` series, `docs/spikes/ZOD-EPH-004-moon-derivative.md`), built in the ecliptic of the mean equinox of date and rotated to ECT by the library's own fixed chain, applied to the position and to the velocity alike. The 114-term table is generated from the vendored source into `native/astronomy_c/zodiann_moon_terms.h` by `tool/gen_moon_terms.dart` (a host test regenerates it and requires byte equality), so the remaining constants (about 100 by the reviewer's count: the `Init` polynomials, the `FAC` factors, `LongPeriodic` about 44, `Planetary` about 33, and the latitude and distance formulas) are hand-copied, and the value-regression test covers them. Trade-off: about 400 lines of our own C instead of 80, tested only on a device, plus two host guards.

The reviewer's circular-orbit model (light time only, scratch script, not in the repository) gave that third term as Mercury about 6.8e-4, Venus about 1.95e-4, Mars about 1.7e-5, Jupiter about 7e-6 °/day (outer planets smaller still). The model is an estimate, not a measurement. Sufficient means all of: (a) `|astronomy speed − Swiss speed|` ≤ **1.2e-3 °/day** for Mercury, ≤ **4.5e-4** for Venus, ≤ **3e-4** for the Sun, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune and Pluto, and, for the Moon, the figure the owner freezes after the diagnostic run (the earlier 0.005 was a placeholder and is not an acceptance threshold; the diagnostic run asserts nothing for the Moon). **The Moon's speed is verified in layers, not by Swiss alone:** the project-authored derivative is proved against upstream's own series by the value regression and the integral identity (Task 3 Step 1b: the value regression to 1e-11, the integral identity to 1e-9 degrees with R to 1e-12 AU, plus the rotation invariants, so a derivative or rotation bug larger than the stated tolerances cannot hide), and the Swiss comparison then measures what remains, the difference between two lunar theories plus the omitted terms of D1 (expected 1e-4 to 1e-3 °/day, an estimate), which the owner's frozen bound will reflect. The Moon has no stations, so (c) does not apply to it, at every sampled instant. (b) the sign of the speed (retrograde) equals Swiss's at every sampled instant where `|Swiss speed|` exceeds the **fixed bound of (a)** for that body (not the measured maximum, which would widen its own exemption); (c) at each station the zero-crossing instant of the astronomy speed is within **1 day** of Swiss's, and the offset is reported. - **D10 (the Earth observer's velocity; DECIDED by the owner, 2026-11-21: E0 first, then E2 only if the effect is confirmed; E2 was explicitly approved on that condition; the D7 thresholds are never adjusted to accommodate a failure; found during the Moon feasibility reading; a hypothesis from reading the source, not a measurement).** The library is internally inconsistent about what the VSOP "Earth" point is. `Astronomy_BaryState(BODY_EARTH)` returns `CalcVsopPosVel(vsop[BODY_EARTH])`, and `HelioVector`, `BaryState` (for the Moon) and `GeoVector` (through `BackdatePosition`) use that point as the Earth's centre, but the gravity-simulation code passes `EARTH_GM + MOON_GM` for the same point (astronomy.c line 3912), which treats it as the Earth–Moon barycentre. The VSOP tables carry no label, so which one it is **is not established by line 3912 alone**. **If it is the barycentre,** the offset to the Earth's centre has a period of 27.3 days and a velocity of about 12.4 m/s (4670 km × 2π / 27.32 d; about 7.2e-6 AU/day), which shifts the longitude rate of a body at distance d AU by up to about 4.1e-4/d °/day: about 4.2e-4 for the Sun, up to about 1.5e-3 for Venus (d about 0.27 AU), up to about 1.1e-3 for Mars (d about 0.37 AU) and up to about 7.5e-4 for Mercury (d about 0.55 AU, below its 1.2e-3 bound on this term alone). The Sun, Venus and Mars would then probably exceed their approved bounds (3e-4, 4.5e-4, 3e-4). The library's position would carry the same offset, whose angle scales as 1/d: up to about 6.4/d arcseconds (Sun about 6.4, Mercury about 12, Mars about 17, Venus about 24), all inside the 60-arcsecond ACC-2 bound but using a large part of it for the near planets, so a speed derived consistently from the same point is the derivative of the library's own position but differs from Swiss Ephemeris's true geocentric speed by that amount. Options. **E0 (recommended first step; a measurement, not an implementation):** before choosing, in a scratch directory outside the repository, compile the vendored `astronomy.c` with the host `gcc` and compare the Sun's apparent geocentric ECT longitude over about 60 days of dense samples with a reference series fetched for this purpose (a JPL Horizons OBSERVER query for the Sun, quantity 31, saved as untrusted data in the scratch directory; the repository's own Horizons fixtures are too sparse and the Swiss binding is emulator-only; no repository change), looking for a 27.3-day signal of up to about 6.5 arcseconds correlated with the Moon's phase; this settles the hypothesis cheaply and does not wait for the Task 6 run. **E1 (the approved formulation, unchanged):** run the D7 comparison as approved; if the Sun, Venus, Mars or Mercury exceed their bounds with a 27.3-day oscillation, stop and report (Global Constraints). **E2 (a formula change that needs the owner's explicit approval, not applied):** use the Earth's centre velocity `v_earth = v_vsop − v_moon / (1 + EARTH_MOON_MASS_RATIO)` with the project-authored analytic Moon velocity of this plan (no finite difference), so `v_geo = v_body − v_vsop + v_moon/(1+k)`, applied in ECT after the rotations. (The signal period for the Sun would be synodic, not 27.3 days.)
